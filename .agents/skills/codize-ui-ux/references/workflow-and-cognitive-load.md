# Workflow and cognitive load

## Current change first

- Keep the current change dominant and the broader build plan secondary.
- Show one primary cognitive task and one primary action at a time.
- Avoid stage counts, duplicated status cards, and full-roadmap exposure by default.
- Preserve ambition by sequencing complexity rather than hiding or rejecting the student's larger idea.

## Progressive disclosure

Keep essential context and the next action visible. Disclose optional Why?, Need help?, source detail, advanced controls, and deeper explanations at the moment they become useful.

Progressive disclosure must not hide uncertainty, required warnings, save/error state, or the student's ability to recover.

## Adaptive flow

Do not show every possible habit intervention on every change. The learning engine may ask, teach, remind, or stay quiet. If the student already supplies a bounded outcome, boundary, check, or reasonable effort choice, skip the redundant interaction.

## Recovery

Something broke stays easy to reach. Recovery asks one question at a time, distinguishes known facts from hypotheses, uses repository history when available, and investigates before another patch.

## Responsive behavior

Mobile reduces simultaneous information rather than shrinking everything. Code/diff inspection may become a full-screen subview with a clear route back to the Build conversation.

## V1 maintenance note

Current V1 screens still have separate phase, task, workflow-artifact, gate, and report contracts. Preserve their honest distinctions during scoped maintenance, but do not use them to design the V2 workflow.
